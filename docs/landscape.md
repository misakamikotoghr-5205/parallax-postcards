# Related open-source work and the actual difference

Verified 2026-11-05. First-person raycasting and perspective puzzles have a long history. This game does not claim to invent either. Its bounded goal is a short, readable, software-rendered observation game that works without pointer lock, WebGL or asset downloads.

Reuse boundary: **design research only**, not copied code, textures, room layouts or screenshots. DDA traversal and perspective projection are general mathematical techniques; `world.js` and `renderer.js` are independently written. The two MIT references are not bundled dependencies. If later contributors copy their source, they must retain the relevant notices and license text.

Substantive implementation: each picture consists of actual world-space polyline fragments at independently chosen depths. The capture condition projects every vertex from the current pose, measures its image-space error against the authored picture, and checks wall visibility. Moving away or turning changes alignment; pressing capture at an arbitrary position does not grant progress. The software renderer clips the same geometry against per-column wall depth. An isolated room prototype became a complete three-room sequence with real read/capture requirements, undo, navigation controls and a finished postcard artifact.

Validation: legal-input integration test completes all three rooms without teleporting; all target viewpoints produce 100% agreement; wrong poses, unread letters and occluded views reject capture. Other tests cover ray intersections, collision substeps, terminal collection, bounded undo, restored-save constraints and finite Canvas2D drawing calls. Browser QA is separate. There is no user study, performance comparison or claim of greater general capability than mature engines.
